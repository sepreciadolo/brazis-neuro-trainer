## Possible clinical errors (original left unchanged; decide in the review tool)

- **ch22-002**: Book p. 668 says bilateral ACA-MCA border zone ischemia causes bibrachial impairment often confined to the FOREARMS AND HANDS (distal), plus impaired volitional saccades. The question describes proximal arm and leg weakness with hands and feet preserved ('man-in-a-barrel'); that phrase and the proximal/leg pattern are not in this chapter. The mechanism (bilateral ACA-MCA watershed) matches the book, but the vignette and explanation do not. Flag for the physician: the usual teaching of man-in-the-barrel (proximal brachial diplegia) differs from the book's wording. Book says: "Bilateral border zone ischemia between the ACAs and MCAs results in bibrachial cortical sensorimotor impairment often confined to the forearms and hands"
- **ch22-006**: Book p. 668 describes bilateral ACA-MCA border zone ischemia as bibrachial impairment often confined to forearms and hands (distal); the question and explanation claim proximal shoulder and hip weakness with preserved hands and feet, and the homunculus argument about hand and foot territories is not in the chapter. The territory named (anterior ACA-MCA border zone) agrees with the book, the clinical pattern does not. Book says: "Bilateral border zone ischemia between the ACAs and MCAs results in bibrachial cortical sensorimotor impairment often confined to the forearms and hands"
