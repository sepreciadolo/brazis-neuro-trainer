## Possible clinical errors (original left unchanged; decide in the review tool)

- **ch02-003**: Book p. 47 says ulnar neuropathy at the elbow OFTEN SPARES the flexor carpi ulnaris; the explanation says elbow lesions 'usually weaken the flexor carpi ulnaris and medial flexor digitorum profundus', and its stem uses a normal FCU as evidence for a wrist lesion. Book p. 47-48 also warns that an elbow lesion may preferentially affect the fascicle to distal hand muscles while sparing sensory branches and mimic a Guyon canal lesion. In addition, the stem has dense sensory loss over the hypothenar eminence, but the book (p. 46, 48) puts the palmar cutaneous branch (hypothenar skin) and dorsal branch above the wrist, so a lesion within Guyon canal distal to them spares the hypothenar skin and the dorsum; loss on the hypothenar eminence with a normal dorsum does not fit the marked localisation. The sparing of the dorsal ulnar sensation does support a wrist-level lesion (p. 48). Also the stated '5 to 8 cm proximal to the wrist' is not given in the book (it says 'arises above the wrist'). A replacement question is written. Book says: "Ulnar neuropathy at the elbow often spares the flexor carpi ulnaris muscle."
- **ch02-007**: Book p. 48 (Ebeling group 2): compression of the proximal part of the terminal motor branch within the canal of Guyon gives a PURELY MOTOR syndrome with no sensory loss because it lies distal to the superficial terminal cutaneous branch. The stem has tingling and numbness of the little finger and medial ring finger (sensory symptoms in the territory of the superficial terminal branch), which a deep motor branch lesion cannot cause, so the marked answer does not fit the stem. The explanation also says elbow lesions involve flexor carpi ulnaris (book p. 47: often spares FCU) and that the dorsal cutaneous branch branches 'in the forearm' (book: above the wrist; agrees). 'Zone II' is not in the book. A replacement question is written. Book says: "In these purely motor lesions, all the hand muscles innervated by the ulnar nerve are affected."
