## Possible clinical errors (original left unchanged; decide in the review tool)

- **ch15-015**: Book p. 453: Claude syndrome (more dorsal midbrain tegmental lesion injuring dorsal red nucleus and brachium conjunctivum) produces 'similar clinical manifestations' to Benedikt (oculomotor paresis, contralateral ataxia/tremor, contralateral hemiparesis) with prominent cerebellar signs and NO hemiballismus. The explanation instead says Claude 'lacks the hemiparesis and the tremor' and the keyed option invents a 'substantia nigra/ventral tegmentum chorea' discriminator; the book states neither. Also the Nothnagel distractor is described as 'bilateral inferior colliculi' with gait ataxia, whereas the book (p. 453) defines Nothnagel as unilateral or bilateral CN III paralysis with contralateral cerebellar ataxia, which is nearly the same picture as the keyed answer, making the item ambiguous. Replacement (new question 1) written. Book says: "but with prominent cerebellar signs (e.g., asynergia, ataxia, dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia) and no hemiballismus"
